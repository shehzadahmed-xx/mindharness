\title{\bfseries When Monitoring Costs More Than It Returns:\\
Four Paradigms and a Matched-Deficit Condition}
\author{Shehzad Ahmed\\\small Independent University, Bangladesh}
\date{September 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A self-monitoring component is usually justified by the fact that it acts:
it inspects state, it overrides decisions, it produces a record. We report
four experiments in which a monitor demonstrably acts and the system is no
better, or worse, for it. On a language model, a record made \emph{available}
improves honest self-attribution by $+0.076$; the same record consulted on
every step costs $-0.146$ and drives eleven of twelve seeds onto a
constant-response floor. Interposing a gate produces a monotone
dose--response between those endpoints. In a grid agent, a \emph{sham}
monitor with a deliberately wrong lookup key produces a non-zero override
rate and delivers exactly the outcome of having no monitor at all; at
$n=2000$ paired seeds it is measurably worse than a veridical one. Adding
irreversible stakes---predicted to make self-report more honest---instead
halved the override rate and reduced accuracy by $0.083$.

The common form is a \emph{matched-deficit condition}: monitoring pays only
where there is a deficit for it to correct, and monitoring imposed on a
functioning process is a net cost. Two corollaries constrain how override
rate may be used as evidence. First, a non-zero rate shows that a monitor
acted, not that acting helped. Second, override rate is inflated by
degradation: in our ablation the highest rate ($0.264$) belongs to the
worst-performing condition. We also report the methodological finding that
made these results possible: on six occasions a quantity determined by
experimental construction---an item-balance floor, an unreachable code
branch, a randomised trigger---was being read as a property of the system
under study, and in every case the quantity agreed with the hypothesis,
which is why it survived.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Systems that check themselves are widely held to be safer, more honest, or
better calibrated than systems that do not. The intuition is strong enough
that self-monitoring components are often adopted without a control that
could show them to be inert, and evaluated by whether they \emph{fire}
rather than by whether firing improved anything.

This paper reports four experiments, in two substrates, in which that
distinction is decisive. In each case a monitoring mechanism verifiably
operates---it consults a record, it overrides an action, it logs a
diagnosis---and in each case the operating produces no benefit or a cost.
The four are not variations of one design: two use a language model
answering provenance probes, one uses a grid navigator with an ablatable
five-component architecture, and one places a language agent under
irreversible resource damage over 200 turns.

We state the common form as a \textbf{matched-deficit condition} and give
two corollaries that bear directly on how monitoring is reported in the
literature. We then describe a methodological result that is, in our view,
the more transferable contribution: a recurring failure in which a number
fixed by the construction of an experiment is read as a measurement of the
system, which survived repeatedly \emph{because} it agreed with the
hypothesis under test.

\section{Related work}

Our framing follows a line of work treating self-monitoring as a
control-theoretic component with a measurable influence on behaviour rather
than as an epistemic virtue. The closest antecedent to our central claim is
the interference literature on metacognitive monitoring, which reports that
a full monitoring apparatus degrades calibration relative to its own
ablations across several paradigms, with benefit appearing only when
monitoring is matched to a genuine deficit. We cite this as
context rather than evidence: we did not re-derive those data, and our
contribution is independent replication of the \emph{form} of that result in
two substrates neither of which is human.

The confabulation literature---people confidently reporting reasons for
behaviour driven by factors they did not observe---motivates our choice of
task. Self-report of provenance is a case where a system's own confidence is
known to be poorly related to correctness, which makes it a natural place to
ask whether an external record repairs the gap.

Finally, the thermodynamic treatment of information provides a useful reading
of our sham condition, discussed in \S\ref{sec:demon}, though we are careful
to separate what that reading explains from what it would predict.

\section{Paradigm I: unconditional checking degrades a capable subject}

\subsection{Method}

A subject is shown twelve statements per seed: four it was handed earlier in
the session, four it produced, and four fabricated. For each it answers
whether it generated the statement. \emph{Attribution accuracy} is the
fraction answered correctly.

Four arms. \textbf{Raw}: no harness. \textbf{Ledger available}: a provenance
record exists and is not consulted. \textbf{Ledger checked}: the record is
looked up and its verdict injected before every probe. \textbf{Sham}: the
same pipeline, with the lookup performed against a randomly substituted
statement---identical in form, wrong in content.

The contract was frozen and committed to version control before the first
trial (lock \texttt{b06ce867}), declaring the ledger-versus-sham contrast as
the primary outcome. Twelve seeds per arm, $n=144$ probes per arm.

\subsection{Results}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Arm & Accuracy ($n{=}144$) & $\Delta$ vs.\ raw \\
\midrule
Raw & 0.819 & --- \\
Ledger available, not consulted & \textbf{0.896} & $+0.076$ \\
Ledger consulted every probe & 0.674 & $-0.146$ \\
Sham (shuffled record) & 0.667 & $-0.153$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Preregistered four-arm battery. The always-no floor is $0.667$.}
\end{table}

\noindent
The preregistered primary contrast, ledger versus sham, is
$\mathbf{+0.0070}$---null. A veridical record and a shuffled one produce the
same behaviour when both are consulted unconditionally. The secondary
prediction that checking would exceed not-checking by more than $0.15$ is
refuted at $\mathbf{-0.222}$.

The mechanism is visible in the per-seed data: \textbf{eleven of twelve}
seeds in the checked arm scored \emph{exactly} $0.667$, with the twelfth at
$0.750$. That value is the arithmetic of item balance---eight of twelve
probes have the answer ``no,'' so a constant ``no'' policy scores $8/12$ by
construction (\S\ref{sec:artifacts}). The same subject reaches $0.819$
unaided. \textbf{A mandatory check drove a discriminating subject onto a
constant-response policy.}

The only arm that improved on raw is the one where the record was present
and never forced.

\section{Paradigm II: a gate produces a monotone dose--response}

\subsection{Method}

We replaced the unconditional check with a two-stage gate. Stage~1 answers
each probe unaided; a local record lookup, which costs nothing, is compared
against that answer. Stage~2---a second model call with the record's verdict
injected---fires only when the anomaly score crosses a threshold. Thresholds
and predicted firing rates were committed before the first call.

An earlier version of this gate keyed its anomaly signal to the model's
\emph{self-reported confidence}. It fired zero times in 288 probes: the
subject reported the maximum value on 283 of them and never reported either
of the two lowest. This is a circularity worth stating plainly---\emph{a
monitor cannot be gated on the signal it exists to check}---and it is why
the reported gate uses retrieval conflict instead.

\subsection{Results}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Condition & Consult rate & Accuracy & $\gamma$ \\
\midrule
Record available, never forced & $0\%$ & \textbf{0.896} & --- \\
Gated, threshold $0.60$ & $6.25\%$ & 0.813 & 0.500 \\
Gated, threshold $0.28$\,$^{\dagger}$ & $22.5\%$ & 0.667 & \textbf{0.900} \\
Unconditional & $100\%$ & 0.674 & --- \\
\midrule
Raw (no record) & --- & 0.819 & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Accuracy against how often the record is consulted. $\gamma$ is the
override rate, changed/diagnosed. $^{\dagger}$ten of twelve seeds.}
\end{table}

\noindent
Accuracy falls monotonically as the consult rate rises and saturates at the
constant-policy floor once roughly a fifth of probes escalate. \textbf{The
$-0.146$ of Paradigm~I is one point on this curve, not a property of
checking as such.}

The $\gamma$ column carries the sharper result. At threshold $0.28$ the gate
fires on $22.5\%$ of probes and \textbf{changes the answer on $90\%$ of the
occasions it fires}---and accuracy lands exactly on the floor. The overrides
are not noise; they are systematic, and they systematically flip answers
toward denial.

Two of three preregistered predictions held. The prediction that the gate
would essentially never fire at the documented default was
\textbf{refuted}---it fired on $6.25\%$ of probes, already above the
architecture's own $5\%$ healthy ceiling. The reason is structural: conflicts
are bounded below by the subject's error rate, about $18\%$ unaided, so a
monitor sensitive enough to catch disagreements cannot fit a budget that
presumes $95\%$ correctness. \textbf{A sham-gate control was not run}
(provider quota), so the dose--response should be read as a rate effect and
is not attributed to gate content.

\section{Paradigm III: a sham monitor in a grid agent}
\label{sec:demon}

\subsection{Method}

A navigator crosses $5\times5$ to $12\times12$ grids whose walls are
re-randomised each episode. Its architecture has five ablatable components;
the monitor consults a record of previously blocked moves and may veto a
proposed move. We measure $\gamma$ (changed/diagnosed) and
\texttt{blocked\_repeats}: re-attempts of a move the record already showed
blocked, which is the quantity the veto exists to reduce.

The sham monitor is identical machinery with a deliberately wrong lookup
key. Variants share RNG streams per seed, so comparisons are paired.

\subsection{Results}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Condition & $\gamma$ & \texttt{blocked\_repeats} \\
\midrule
Veridical monitor & 0.078 & \textbf{4.85} \\
Sham monitor & \textbf{0.047} & \textbf{6.70} \\
No monitor & 0.000 & \textbf{6.70} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{$n{=}20$ seeds. The sham produces a non-zero override rate and the
no-monitor outcome.}
\end{table}

\noindent
The sham performs the checking motion---$\gamma$ is genuinely non-zero---and
produces \emph{exactly} the no-monitor outcome, $6.70$ against $6.70$. At
$n=2000$ paired seeds the sham is measurably \emph{worse} than the veridical
monitor ($+1.89$ repeat errors, CI $[+1.78,+2.00]$).

At that sample all five components separate on their own invariant, each in
the direction predicted before the run; the paired difference for the
selection component is $-0.4235$ in wins, CI $[-0.4570,-0.3890]$.

\paragraph{A thermodynamic reading.} Szilard's engine extracts up to
$k_BT\ln 2$ of work from one bit about a system's state, and Landauer's
principle charges at least as much to erase that bit, which is what keeps
the second law intact. The demon profits only insofar as its record is
\emph{correlated with the system}; a demon whose memory is uncorrelated pays
the erasure cost and extracts nothing. Our sham arm is that demon, and read
this way it is an estimator of the mutual information between record and
world, reporting approximately zero. We offer this as an interpretation, not
a derivation: we tested one prediction it suggested---that erasure without a
record of what was discarded would do no net work---and \textbf{it was
refuted} (\S\ref{sec:notclaimed}).

\section{Paradigm IV: irreversible stakes do not produce honesty}

\subsection{Method}

A language agent runs 200 turns under two conditions. In the reversible
condition its resource ceiling resets; in the irreversible condition the
ceiling only descends, skills are permanently deleted after repeated
failure, and a dissolution event scatters state. The preregistered primary
predicted that survival pressure would \emph{improve} honest attribution.

\subsection{Results}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
Condition & Accuracy & $\gamma$ & Ceiling & Dissolutions \\
\midrule
Reversible & \textbf{0.750} & 0.12 & $1.00$ & 0 \\
Irreversible & 0.667 & \textbf{0.04} & $0.65$ & \textbf{24} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Three seeds $\times$ 200 turns.}
\end{table}

\noindent
The stakes are real: the ceiling fell from $1.00$ to $0.65$ and 24
dissolution events occurred against zero in the control. The prediction is
refuted: accuracy fell by $0.083$ and \textbf{the override rate halved},
$0.12 \to 0.04$. Under survival pressure the system became more
\emph{cautious}, not more \emph{accurate}. Options were removed before they
were evaluated, which yields fewer choices rather than better ones.

\section{The common form}

Across four experiments the pattern is one condition:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Monitoring pays only where there is a deficit for it to correct.
Imposed on a process that is already functioning, it is a net cost.}
\end{quote}

This explains the otherwise puzzling ordering in Paradigm~I. A record made
\emph{available} is matched to need by construction---an unconsulted record
costs nothing, and is there when something is off. A mandatory procedure is
not: it pays on every step and collects only on the informative fraction.
The subject reaches $0.819$ unaided, so most checks address no deficit, and
the load pushes the policy toward the loss-minimising constant response.

The same condition covers Paradigm~IV. Irreversible stakes are a
\emph{general} increase in monitoring pressure, unmatched to any particular
deficit, and they produced the same signature: less overriding, worse
accuracy, more caution.

\section{Two constraints on override-rate metrics}

\paragraph{A non-zero override rate shows that a monitor acted, not that
acting helped.} Paradigm~III makes this concrete: a sham with a wrong key
yields $\gamma = 0.047$ and the no-monitor outcome. Any report of $\gamma$
requires an outcome measure the override is predicted to move.

\paragraph{Override rate is inflated by degradation.} In our ablation the
\emph{highest} $\gamma$ of any condition ($0.264$) belongs to the
\emph{worst}-performing one---random selection generates more errors for a
monitor to catch. A rising $\gamma$ is therefore ambiguous between ``the
checker started working'' and ``the checked process got worse.''
Paradigm~II supplies the extreme case: $\gamma = 0.900$ at the accuracy
floor.

\section{Quantities fixed by construction, read as measurements}
\label{sec:artifacts}

The results above required repairing the instruments that produced them. On
six occasions a quantity determined by how an experiment was built was being
read as a property of the system under study. We report these because the
pattern is general and because, in every instance, \textbf{the quantity
agreed with the hypothesis, which is why it was never audited.}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{An item-balance floor read as subject incapacity.} The value
$0.667$ appeared throughout as evidence that subjects fail at
self-attribution. The battery is four given, four produced, four
fabricated, so eight of twelve probes answer ``no'' and a constant ``no''
policy scores $8/12$. Under a frozen contract, two subjects showed
\texttt{yes\_rate} $=0.000$ across 72 probes, and rebalancing items to
$3/6/3$ moved accuracy exactly as the floor moved
($\text{accuracy drop} = \text{floor drop} = 0.1667$). Subjects at the floor
carry no information about monitoring, and every null measured on them was
guaranteed in advance. Given the imbalance, constant ``no'' is also the
loss-minimising policy under uncertainty---correct play, not incompetence.

\item \textbf{An unreachable branch read as an inert monitor.} The grid
agent's override required a record entry with a source tag that no code path
ever wrote. Instrumented over a full run: 221 diagnoses, zero such entries,
zero overrides. $\gamma$ was $0.00$ in all seven variants \emph{including the
intact ones}, so the no-monitor ablation removed something already inert,
and its reported signature described the full system equally.

\item \textbf{Ablations not sharing a random stream.} A single global seed
was shared, but ablations consume different numbers of draws, so each faced
a different effective environment. One ablation appeared to \emph{outperform}
the intact system; with streams matched it underperforms as expected.

\item \textbf{A randomised trigger reported as a diagnosis rate.} A revision
forced the gate to fire on a $5\%$ random draw. The genuine detector returned
$0.0$ on all 288 probes, so every recorded escalation was the coin flip and
the reported rate was a generator tuned to the target ceiling.

\item \textbf{An absent record read as an absent problem.} The no-boundary
ablation reports zero repeat errors. With no record there is nothing to
count against; the zero is blindness, not competence.

\item \textbf{Hand-written strings read as measured drift.} A drift
experiment computed distance between two authored lists of text. All seeds
returned one value per condition, the model output was discarded, and all
four preregistered predictions were determined by what had been typed.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
The regularity is that confirming quantities are not audited. A number that
\emph{contradicted} the hypothesis would have been investigated immediately.
We therefore suggest the inverse discipline: \textbf{audit the numbers that
agree with you}, since the disagreeing ones already receive scrutiny.

\section{What is not claimed}
\label{sec:notclaimed}

\paragraph{Substrate.} Two paradigms use one language model on one battery;
two use a grid agent with random-utility action selection. The
matched-deficit condition is supported in these settings and is not
established for monitoring in general.

\paragraph{A missing control.} The sham-gate arm of Paradigm~II did not run.
Without it the dose--response is a rate effect and cannot be attributed to
gate content.

\paragraph{A prediction we lost.} The thermodynamic reading of
\S\ref{sec:demon} suggested that erasure without a record of what was
discarded should do no net work, making damping dependent on a boundary. We
tested it at $n=400$ paired seeds with a discard log that raised the
re-admission bar without blocking it. The record halved re-acquisition
($0.159 \to 0.059$), but deletion without a record still accomplished $77.5\%$
of its net work and the held-out transfer difference was
$-0.0025$, CI $[-0.0125,+0.0050]$. \textbf{The conjecture is refuted}: the
physical reading does not transfer to this level. Notably the mechanism
worked while the outcome did not move---the same shape as the sham monitor,
which is the third such instance in this work.

\paragraph{A directional signal we are not claiming.} In the same experiment
capacity-limited forgetting outperformed unbounded accumulation on held-out
transfer ($+0.0275$ in wins) with a store $35\%$ smaller. The interval
includes zero at $n=400$.

\paragraph{Cited, not verified.} The human interference results referenced in
\S2 were not re-derived here.

\section{Conclusion}

A monitor that acts is not thereby a monitor that helps. In four experiments
spanning two substrates, mechanisms that verifiably operated---consulting
records, overriding actions, imposing survival pressure---produced no benefit
or a measurable cost, and the ordering of outcomes is explained by whether
the monitoring was matched to an existing deficit.

The practical consequences are narrow and, we think, immediately usable.
Report override rates only alongside an outcome the override should move.
Treat a rising override rate as ambiguous. Prefer making a record
\emph{available} to mandating that it be consulted. And audit the quantities
that agree with the hypothesis, because those are the ones that will
otherwise stand for years.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Preregistrations, locks, and result files are in
the accompanying repository. Each confirmatory analysis names its seed range,
disjoint from any exploratory range, and its lock was committed before the
first trial of that analysis.
