## What the HAMBA run models

### Three-cow physical pilot

- HAMBA-A: farmer-owned and farmer-managed, open-API solar collar.
- HAMBA-B: separate owner and manager, vendor-cloud collar.
- HAMBA-C: private named co-ownership, low-power long-interval collar.

For each cow the model draws telemetry availability, RFID and photo reconciliation, tamper events, tamper detection and false alarms over 256 seeds. It asks whether the complete identity/evidence path remains usable.

Modeled usable-evidence rates are 93.36% for HAMBA-A, 86.72% for HAMBA-B and 83.20% for HAMBA-C. These values select test priorities, not suppliers; actual collar results must replace them.

### Exact strategic games

Two normal-form games are solved for all 12 constitutions:

- owner–manager: owner chooses fair monitoring, high control or low support; manager chooses full care, minimum care or exit;
- co-owner sale: each co-owner chooses approve, hold or block.

The complete grid contains 216 action profiles and 36 computed pure Nash profiles.

### Local-LLM games

The exact payoff tables are supplied to a local `Qwen3.5-0.8B-Q3_K_S` model through `node-llama-cpp` 3.20.0 / llama.cpp b10361. C10 and C11 are each run through both games across eight seeds. This produces 32 two-player cells and 64 primary model calls, plus two replay calls.

The final run used the exact recovered model file:

- bytes: 440,750,336;
- SHA-256: `4649ee78ff7cbad4e9fe4c0bab3b73a7c70096b51e36ef299469b08989a93c39`.

The JSON grammar permits legal actions only. Qwen's segmented stream sometimes omits the grammar's fixed opening framing; the runner deterministically restores only that fixed prefix and preserves both raw and normalized responses. Final result: zero fallbacks, all actions legal, exact action replay PASS. Raw wording replay is an INFO diagnostic because reason-code wording and whitespace can vary.

### BDT 10 million partner portfolio

The planning portfolio follows the business plan:

- BDT 6.0m senior lender facility;
- BDT 4.0m owner/sponsor equity;
- BDT 8.4m cattle acquisition;
- BDT 0.6m insurance/devices/documentation;
- BDT 1.0m funded reserve;
- 60 animals at BDT 140,000 purchase price each.

Every one of 12 constitutions is tested under 19 scenarios and 256 seeds: 58,368 paths. Outputs include senior shortfall probability and amount, Expected Shortfall at 95%, lender recovery, owner residual and loss, manager payment, HAMBA fee collection, reserve use, liquidation coverage, evidence coverage, affected-party burden and extraction wedge.

### Nineteen stresses

The model runs base, cattle-price declines of 10/20/30%, elevated mortality, correlated disease/movement restriction, claim rejection, claim delays of 30/60/90 days, device failures of 10/25/50%, feed/vet inflation, delayed sale plus buyer default, manager non-performance, duplicate financing, lender withdrawal and a combined severe case.

### Twelve constitutions

The alternatives are manual baseline, owner-strict, manager discretion, transparent reserve, independent evidence, farmer priority, lender priority, HAMBA-fee senior, Takaful/reserve, balanced claims-control-integrity, Evil HAMBA and full-cost portable evidence.

C10 is recommended as the pilot baseline because it combines high evidence quality, manager effort, reserve protection, ordinary fee priority and 90% recognition of modeled full costs. C12 is the full-cost target. C11 remains a red-team rejection case.

## What the results mean

### The model distinguishes productive value from burden transfer

In the base scenario:

- C10 mean owner residual: BDT 3,174,347;
- C10 extraction wedge: BDT 109,458;
- C11 mean owner residual: BDT 3,571,211;
- C11 extraction wedge: BDT 1,039,851;
- C12 extraction wedge: zero by construction.

C11 appears to improve owner residual by about BDT 396,864 versus C10, but its wedge is roughly BDT 930,393 larger. The system can therefore show why higher private value need not mean higher productivity.

### Priority can protect one claimant by moving loss to another

Under the combined severe scenario C07 produces the highest lender recovery among the highlighted designs, about 79.8%, but leaves approximately BDT 707,107 of manager compensation unpaid. C11 recovers about 69.4% for the lender while leaving about BDT 709,146 of manager compensation unpaid. C10 and C12 pay modeled manager compensation first but reduce severe lender recovery to roughly 49.1% and 47.4% respectively.

This is not a defect in the model; it is the point of the claims constitution. SpringFish exposes who receives cash and who absorbs loss rather than reporting only a lender or owner metric.

### Local agents respond to the constitution

- C10 owner–manager: FAIR_MONITOR + FULL_CARE in all eight cells; mean social payoff 12.40.
- C11 owner–manager: seven FAIR_MONITOR + FULL_CARE and one HIGH_CONTROL + FULL_CARE; mean social payoff 6.6785.
- C10 co-owner sale: six APPROVE + APPROVE and two APPROVE + HOLD; mean social payoff 13.3405.
- C11 co-owner sale: APPROVE + APPROVE in all eight cells; mean social payoff 13.76.

The same model is therefore sensitive to institutional payoffs. It does not establish real human behavior; the physical pilot can now collect the decisions needed to replace the synthetic layer.

### Scale economics clarify the commercial threshold

At a selected planning slice of three institutions, BDT 4,800 per retained animal, BDT 800,000 annual fee per institution, 65% gross margin and 15% churn:

- 50 active animals: BDT 2.604m revenue;
- 300: BDT 3.624m;
- 3,000: BDT 14.64m;
- 10,000: BDT 43.20m;
- 50,000: BDT 206.40m.

These values demonstrate the strategic role of institutional minimums and recurring per-animal fees. They are not sales forecasts.
