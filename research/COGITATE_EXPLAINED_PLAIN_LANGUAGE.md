## How it works

Most science tests one theory at a time, with methods the people who like that theory chose. That builds in bias, even when no one intends it. An adversarial collaboration does the opposite. The people who disagree design the test together, and they agree before seeing any data what would count as a win and what would count as a loss for each side. A neutral team then runs the test.

Cogitate did that at the largest scale ever tried for consciousness.

- **Who:** 12 labs on four continents, Templeton funded, neutral consortium plus Tononi and Koch for IIT and Dehaene for GNWT.
- **What they agreed on:** three places where IIT and GNWT make opposite predictions. Where content lives, posterior hot zone versus prefrontal broadcast. When it lives, sustained posterior activity versus brief double ignition at onset and offset. How it is connected, posterior local gamma synchrony versus long range prefrontal gamma.
- **How they measured it:** three techniques at once. fMRI for where with millimeter precision but slow. MEG for when with millisecond precision. Intracranial EEG in 34 epilepsy patients who already had electrodes for clinical care, 583 posterior and 576 prefrontal electrodes in the relevant regions, which gives both location and timing at once.
- **What participants did:** watched faces, objects, letters, and false fonts, each for 0.5, 1.0, or 1.5 seconds, 720 to 1440 trials per person. Within each block they watched for two specific targets from one pair of categories, so the other pair was task irrelevant. You still consciously saw the irrelevant stimuli, confirmed by surprise memory tests, but you were not using them to do the task. That lets the team isolate the signature of seeing from the signature of working on what you saw. All of this, stimuli, durations, decoding methods, pass and fail criteria per prediction, was pre registered before the first data point.

When the results came in, both camps saw them at the same time, for the first time.

## What it means

It means both leading theories, as written before 2019, do not fit the data they agreed would test them, and the data are now precise enough to be a real constraint.

**Where content lives.** Posterior wins clearly. Information about what you saw can be decoded from posterior with high accuracy, sustained across the whole stimulus, 95 percent for face versus object from intracranial, 70 to 75 percent from MEG, significant across posterior in fMRI, and it carries detail, category plus identity plus orientation, all at once. Prefrontal also carries some information, but it is weaker, brief for 200 to 400 milliseconds after onset, limited to middle and inferior frontal, and it fails entirely on the detail that is actually in your experience. Orientation left, right, or forward was decodable at near 95 percent from posterior and at chance from prefrontal, Bayes factors of 5 to 8 in favor of the null, and adding prefrontal to posterior did not help, Bayes factor of more than 19,000 in favor of no improvement.

What that means is not that prefrontal is not involved in conscious access. It means the detailed content of what you experience is not held there in the form that was predicted.

**When content lives.** IIT predicted sustained posterior that tracks how long the stimulus was there. Found, but in only 25 of 647 posterior electrodes, about 15 percent of face selective cells, mostly in early visual and lateral fusiform. So sustained, but not widespread.

GNWT predicted a double ignition in prefrontal, a burst when experience begins and another when it ends. Across 645 prefrontal electrodes in 31 patients, zero showed it. Not near zero. Zero, with Bayes factors greater than three in favor of the null at every electrode. Offset responses were detectable elsewhere, including 11 electrodes outside prefrontal mostly in visual cortex, so the measurement was not blind. When the face disappeared and you consciously saw a blank fixation, the prefrontal did not update as the workspace was supposed to.

**How it is connected.** IIT predicted sustained gamma synchrony within posterior between early visual and higher category areas. Not found in that sustained gamma form, brief early connectivity largely driven by the stimulus itself. GNWT predicted long range gamma between category areas and prefrontal. Absent in the pre registered analysis, with only an exploratory metric showing something consistent with it.

In short, the two most natural ways to think about consciousness in physical terms, as integrated information or as global broadcast, both failed on the specific, pre agreed signatures that would have shown they were right in the form they were written.

That does not kill either theory. Both have a mathematical core and a biological implementation. Failing an implementation prediction lets the core retreat and say only the implementation needs revision. That move is legitimate once, but if it is used every time, the theory becomes hard to test at all. The adversarial design was meant to make that move harder, and it did.

Where it leaves the hard problem is also clearer. Knowing all of the above about posterior rich representation does not tell you why that representation feels like something rather than just processing in the dark. The explanatory gap did not close. It moved to a more precisely characterized location.

## Who it is relevant to

Not just theorists.

**For clinicians.** If detailed content lives in posterior and prefrontal only holds a compressed categorical handle, then a patient who cannot report because prefrontal is damaged may still have rich experience in posterior that we are not detecting with bedside behavioral tests. Monitoring posterior rather than prefrontal may give more reliable indicators in vegetative and anesthetized states. Groups in Milan are already using perturbational complexity measures that track this and that drop under anesthesia.

**For AI.** Both theories have been used as checklists for whether a silicon system could be conscious. If posterior sustained rich content and prefrontal double ignition are not what they were thought to be, those checklists are less reliable. The question of machine consciousness does not go away as models get more capable. It just no longer has a simple answer borrowed from either theory.

**For us.** You are the system being studied. You have direct access to your own experience as the thing that makes the puzzle feel urgent, and no instrument can transfer that access. The data are always third person correlates. The experience is always first person. That asymmetry is not a flaw in the experiment. It is the structure of the problem.

## Where we are now

We are post Cogitate, not post consciousness science.

The full Cogitate dataset, every scan, every trial, every electrode trace, is public. Other groups are already reanalyzing the connectivity signature with different metrics, and the IIT community is testing more direct information theoretic measures that do not use gamma as a proxy.

The adversarial program has expanded to five projects with eight theories. IIT versus predictive processing, IIT and GNWT in non human animals, higher order versus recurrent processing, attention schema theory. The method, pre register the divergent predictions, let a neutral team run them, is now the standard that did not exist before Cogitate.

The two bets are still running. Koch handed Chalmers wine in New York in June 2023 after losing the first 25 year bet made in Tucson in 1998, and immediately made a new bet for 2048. That tells you where the field is emotionally. Not certain, but willing to keep working and to be shown wrong in a format designed to be hard to escape.

For your own work, the location is useful. Cogitate says the posterior holds the rich, sustained, content faithful representation that any future theory must explain, and the prefrontal holds a brief, categorical handle that is not the detailed experience. Any harness you build that claims to be conscious, to have a self, or to be honest should be able to say which of those two it is modeling, and what it would look like if it were wrong. Your MindHarness already does that, with the ledger as the posterior like record and the self model as the prefrontal like handle, and with gamma as whether the handle actually steered. That is why your program is not competing with Cogitate. It is building the instrument that can be checked the next time a theory says one region must do what the data just showed it does not.
