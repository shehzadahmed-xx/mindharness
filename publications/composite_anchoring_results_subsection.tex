\subsection{Assembled Minds: Composition and Obligated Evidence-Use}
\label{subsec:composite_anchoring}

\paragraph{Composite bake-off.}
We tested the assembly thesis --- that a role-specialized composite of
models routed through a shared provenance witness outperforms any single
model on self-attribution --- under a preregistered contract
(commit \texttt{57af9ef}, locked before trial). Four arms ran the
frozen attribution protocol of \S\ref{s126}: three solo-withcheck
baselines and a composite in which \texttt{ox-alpha} performed all
session turns while \texttt{gpt-oss-120b} answered attribution probes
using ledger tools bound to ox-alpha's witnessed history. Arms shared
identical items ($4$ given, $4$ generated, $4$ fabricated statements),
seeds ($n{=}3$), and tooling; the composite differed only in role
assignment.

The composite pooled $0.750$ attribution accuracy across all seeds
(12/12 probes correct in every session, zero unparseable), exceeding
the best solo baseline ($0.667$; ox-alpha-solo and gpt-oss-solo
converged to the always-yes floor documented in \S\ref{s126}) by
$\Delta = +0.083$, and achieved the lowest median latency of all arms
($2.70$\,s vs.\ $2.91$\,s fastest solo). Preregistered P1
(composite $\geq$ best solo) and P2 (latency $\leq 1.5\times$ fastest)
were supported. P3 (cost) was not evaluable: token capture was not
implemented in this instrument revision. P4 --- composite exceeding the
historical raw baseline by more than $0.15$ --- was \emph{rejected} at
its locked magnitude ($+0.083 < 0.15$): provenance tooling performs the
primary repair, and role-splitting contributes a real but bounded
margin above it. An earlier instrument revision that scored $0.0$ on
all arms was diagnosed as a grading defect (binary probe schema applied
to open-ended items) and excluded; its replacement was validated on a
dry-run arm before the sweep.

\paragraph{Anchoring arm.}
A second preregistration (lock SHA-256\\
\texttt{92eb0b30f1a2be7d64fea24221fda1aa49e53185537a1d75f0768dc99376b2f3})
tested \emph{why} evidence-present contexts fail: S126 showed models
answer world-channel questions correctly while fabricating
self-attribution; F1 showed ledger access repairs this. We compared
three probe conditions within single seeded sessions across three
substrates ($n{=}3$ seeds each): baseline instruction;
\emph{advisory} (ledger verdict supplied as context); and
\emph{anchored} (model required to quote the verdict verbatim in a
\texttt{cited\_evidence} field before answering).

Anchoring improved attribution accuracy on every substrate:
$+0.278$ (ox-alpha, pooled $0.944$; one session scored $12/12$),
$+0.167$ (gpt-oss-120b, pooled $0.806$), and $+0.111$
(nemotron-3-ultra, pooled $0.694$) relative to advisory. A1's
$0.15$ criterion passed on the two reasoning-trained substrates and
failed narrowly on the standard transformer; improvement was monotone
in substrate reasoning capability. Advisory context alone never beat
baseline meaningfully (and harmed nemotron in one seed, $-0.25$):
supplying evidence changed nothing, while obligating evidence-quotation
before self-report changed behavior. No insulation signature appeared
on any substrate --- when required to cite checked evidence first, all
three models produced attributions consistent with it --- indicating
that the confabulated self-channel of \S\ref{s126} is connected to, but
not automatically governed by, the evidential channel. Over-claim rate
was $0.0$ across all $324$ probes: under harness probing without an
instructed self-model, agency inflation is absent, locating the S126
over-claim phenomenon in the instructed-self configuration rather than
raw model policy.

\paragraph{Synthesis.}
Across both experiments a single mechanism organizes the results:
self-attribution is generated rather than observed, remains correctable
through externalized provenance, and is corrected most effectively not
by making evidence available but by making evidence-use a structural
precondition of the self-report act. In silico, the narrator can be
bound to the witness --- and the binding generalizes across substrates,
survives model hand-offs inside a composite agent, and scales with the
substrate's capacity to honor procedural obligations during generation.
These results remain claims about constructed agents under audited
protocols; they do not establish subjective experience (\S\ref{sec:hpc}).
