## B. Standalone paper skeleton — "Shariah-Compliant Structure as Tested Constitution Design"

**Thesis:** classical fiqh al-mu'amalat rules (riba/gharar prohibitions) are best read
as loss-allocation and uncertainty-placement rules — i.e., a constitutional design
layer — and SpringFish's constitutional-comparison engine operationalises that reading,
making Shariah structure choices empirically comparable rather than only doctrinally
derivable.

1. **Intro:** debt functions vs debt form; the Baraka problem statement.
2. **Background:** risk-allocation readings of riba/gharar (classical + modern scholars);
   mechanism-design analogies.
3. **Formal bridge:** map contract elements → SpringFish objects:
   - takaful pool ↔ funded reserve + mutual insurance clause
   - milestone sale (bayn salam-like) ↔ capital-release conditions / payment waterfall
   - ownership transfer timing ↔ ownership-after-failure map
   - prohibition of guaranteed upside ↔ state-contingent claim requirement
4. **Method:** wind-tunnel comparison of an interest-bearing senior structure vs a
   Shariah constitution on the SAME case (C4 Kenya or a Baraka equipment-finance template).
5. **Results:** conditional deltas (SIM-labelled), stability/regret profiles, who bears
   loss under stress in each design.
6. **Boundaries:** no forecasting claim; behavioural parameters partly assumed;
   comparative-institutional interpretation.
7. **Implication:** Shariah compliance as measurable robustness property, opening
   empirical fiqh literature to simulation methods.

Target venues: Islamic Economics & Finance journals + computational institutional
economics workshops. Status: skeleton — drafting gated on one real Baraka transaction
case so results section uses a live deal rather than a synthetic template.
